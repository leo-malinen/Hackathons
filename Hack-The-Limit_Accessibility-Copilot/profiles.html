Profiles

Six starting points, none of them a label.

A profile is a set of settings with a name on it. Nothing more. It says what the page should do, never anything about the person reading it. Every one of these is a copy you can edit, and the buttons below are live: they change this page while you sit on it.

The six starter profiles

Applying a profile here changes this whole site straight away. The panel at the bottom right lists what changed, and Restore original puts it all back.

Every control

Or start from nothing and build your own.

These are all of them, grouped the way the panel groups them. Move one and this page moves with it.

Yours to keep

Name it, keep it, hand it over.

A profile is a small piece of text. You can save it under a name, copy it somewhere safe, paste one in that somebody gave you, or delete the lot. Nothing about you goes into it, so it is safe to share.

Save what you have set

Give the settings above a name and they become a profile of your own, sitting beside the six.

Export and import

The text below is your current settings. Copy it to keep it, or paste somebody else's in and press Load.

Where it applies

One site, every site, or just this visit.

A profile can be the way you read everything, or the way you read one site, or something you try for an afternoon and drop. A setting made for one site always beats the global one, and the panel shows you which is winning.

Global

The way you read by default. Every site, until you say otherwise.

This site

Plain Language on news sites but not on a technical reference. Site settings win over global ones.

This visit

Nothing is written down. Close the tab and it is gone. Useful on a shared computer.

You can set the scope in the panel at the bottom right, under Controls.
