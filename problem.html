Chapter one

The financial shock

I can afford this eventually. I cannot afford it today.

Bridge exists in the space between those two sentences. It is a small space, and a great many people live in it.

The arithmetic of one bad Tuesday.

Consider a person earning $3,200 a month with $600 in emergency savings. An unexpected $850 car repair arrives. There is no version of this month where the car stays broken, because the car is how they get to work.

The $850 is covered by the $600, and then it isn't. A $250 gap is left. That gap has to be filled by something, and every available something has a cost attached.

The available options

Seven ways to fill a $250 gap. Each one costs something later.

Use a credit card

Fast, and it works. Revolving a balance turns a one-off cost into a monthly one.

Overdraft the account

A temporary shortage becomes a fee, and fees arrive faster than pay does.

Delay another bill

The gap moves rather than closes, and usually grows a late fee on the way.

Borrow from family

Free, when it is available. Many people have no one to ask.

High-cost emergency credit

Available to almost everyone, priced for the people with the fewest alternatives.

Sell something

Converts an asset into a payment, at whatever price a rushed sale gets.

Cut necessities

Food, heating, medication. The cost lands somewhere that does not appear on a statement.

Structured support

The option Bridge is designed to add: sized to the gap, planned around capacity, with savings attached.

Who this affects

People with income, without liquidity.

Bridge is designed for people who earn but have not been able to build a buffer, which is a different situation from being unable to manage money.

Income can be steady and still arrive unevenly. Essentials can be under control and still leave nothing over. A person can do everything a budgeting app recommends and still be one repair away from a hard month.

The principle

Bridge does not assume that financial stress is caused by irresponsible behaviour.

This matters for product design, not just for tone. A product built on the assumption that the user made a mistake will teach, restrict and moralise. A product built on the assumption that the month was simply harder than expected will measure, support and plan. Bridge is the second kind.

Why existing options fall short

Every current answer solves part of the problem.

None of these are bad products. Each was designed for a job, and covering a sudden essential expense without creating a second problem is not that job.

Emergency savings

The ideal answer, and the one that is missing at the exact moment it is needed. Building a buffer takes months of surplus that the people most exposed to shocks do not have.

Credit cards

Convenient and immediate. A revolving balance can convert a single expense into an ongoing monthly obligation with no end date attached.

Overdrafts

Compound a temporary shortage with fees. The CFPB found the median overdraft fee at large institutions was $35, and that most fees are paid by a small group of people who pay them repeatedly.

Payday and high-cost credit

Available quickly, priced at a median 391 percent APR, with borrowers indebted a median of 199 days a year according to CFPB research. The speed is real. So is the cost.

Traditional loans

Often too slow, too large, or structured for a purchase rather than a shortfall. Few are designed to lend $250 for eight weeks.

Employer assistance

Real programs exist, and many work. They are often fragmented, hard to discover, and operationally heavy for the organisation running them.

Financial education

Improves long-term behaviour and is worth doing. It does not pay the mechanic on Thursday.

Budgeting apps

Excellent at showing where money goes. They describe the emergency accurately and then stop, because resolving it was never their purpose.

Figures above are from the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau. Full citations with links are on the research page.

Why it matters

The cost of an emergency is mostly the second emergency.

A shock that is absorbed badly leaves a household with less capacity than it had before. The next shock then lands on a weaker position, and the one after that on a weaker position still.

Bridge tries to interrupt the chain at the point where the shock happens, because that is the only point where interrupting it is cheap.

Bridge is a concept designed and submitted for the Graphiques Innovation Challenge. It is a proposal, not a product: it is not a live financial service, it does not lend money, it has no users, partners or revenue, and nothing here is financial advice. The Bridge Resilience Profile is a conceptual product framework for financial resilience planning. It is not a credit score and does not represent a lending decision. Every scenario, dashboard and pilot figure on this site is clearly labelled as illustrative. External statistics carry a named source, a year and a link. Any real implementation would require appropriate financial, legal, regulatory, compliance, consumer protection, privacy and risk review.

30 to 45 seconds · The intervention

Most financial products help people transact. Bridge is designed to help people recover.

Traditional credit asks whether a person can repay. Bridge adds a second question: what would help this person recover? That question changes the product from a loan into a recovery plan with support attached.

Twelve weeks with a plan, instead of a year without one.

60 to 75 seconds · The business

Organisations fund resilience. Bridge runs it.

Bridge is business to business to consumer. Employers, universities, nonprofits and financial institutions establish a Resilience Fund and pay for the platform that runs it. Revenue comes from institutional contracts, not from interest charged to people in difficulty.

75 to 90 seconds · The impact

Success is not money distributed. It is people who recovered.

Bridge measures emergency resolution, recovery completion, savings rebuilt, repeat dependence, avoided high-cost credit, and change in the Resilience Profile. A pilot with one employer, one university and one nonprofit could test all six inside twelve months.
