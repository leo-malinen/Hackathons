Research and evidence

Why this problem matters.

Every statistic used anywhere on this site appears below with its source, its year and a link to the original. Where no reliable figure exists, none has been invented.

What this page is not. None of these figures describe Bridge. Bridge has no users, no partners, no revenue and no results. The evidence below describes the problem Bridge is designed for, drawn from government agencies, financial regulators and nonprofit research organisations.

of US households had experienced a financial shock in the previous twelve months, such as a major home or car repair, an illness, or a pay cut.

These figures explain the design constraint rather than making an argument about any individual product. Emergency credit is fast and available, and for a household with no buffer it is often the only thing that is. The cost lands later, and it lands hardest on the people who use it most often.

Workplace emergency savings

Employers including large national companies have begun offering workplace emergency savings accounts funded by payroll deduction, and research from the AARP Public Policy Institute reports associations between employee emergency savings and job satisfaction, productivity and retention. This matters for Bridge because it establishes that organisations already accept a role in the financial resilience of the people they serve.

AARP Public Policy Institute, Emergency Savings initiative. aarp.org · SHRM, Workplace Savings Accounts: Open in Case of Emergency. shrm.org

Method and limits

How this evidence was gathered, and what it does not show.

Being clear about the gaps is part of the argument, not a weakness in it.

  • Sources are limited to government agencies, financial regulators, established financial institutions' research arms, and nonprofit research organisations
  • Figures are quoted from the primary publication rather than from secondary coverage of it
  • Most of this evidence is United States focused, because that is where the strongest public data exists. It should not be assumed to transfer to other countries without checking
  • Some sources are several years old. Where a more recent equivalent exists it has been used, and where it does not the year is stated plainly
  • No figure here measures a Bridge-style intervention, because no such intervention has been run. That evidence would have to come from the pilot
  • Where no reliable statistic was available, the site makes a qualitative claim or says nothing at all, rather than manufacturing a number

Bridge is a concept designed and submitted for the Graphiques Innovation Challenge. It is a proposal, not a product: it is not a live financial service, it does not lend money, it has no users, partners or revenue, and nothing here is financial advice. The Bridge Resilience Profile is a conceptual product framework for financial resilience planning. It is not a credit score and does not represent a lending decision. Every scenario, dashboard and pilot figure on this site is clearly labelled as illustrative. External statistics carry a named source, a year and a link. Any real implementation would require appropriate financial, legal, regulatory, compliance, consumer protection, privacy and risk review.

30 to 45 seconds · The intervention

Most financial products help people transact. Bridge is designed to help people recover.

Traditional credit asks whether a person can repay. Bridge adds a second question: what would help this person recover? That question changes the product from a loan into a recovery plan with support attached.

60 to 75 seconds · The business

Organisations fund resilience. Bridge runs it.

Bridge is business to business to consumer. Employers, universities, nonprofits and financial institutions establish a Resilience Fund and pay for the platform that runs it. Revenue comes from institutional contracts, not from interest charged to people in difficulty.

Success is not money distributed. It is people who recovered.
