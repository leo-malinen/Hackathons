Chapter six

Business model

Organisations fund resilience. Bridge runs it.

Bridge is business to business to consumer. Revenue comes from institutional contracts rather than from interest charged to people in difficulty, which is what lets the product be designed around recovery instead of around repeat borrowing.

Financial sustainability

Three pools of money that never mix.

Keeping these separate is what stops the model from depending on extracting revenue from financially vulnerable people.

Pool one

User money

Money belonging to participants: their savings, their contributions, their repayments. Bridge's revenue never comes out of this pool.

Pool two

Program capital

Capital designated to support qualifying emergencies, held in the sponsoring organisation's Resilience Fund. Recovered support returns here and extends the fund's reach.

Pool three

Bridge revenue

Money earned from institutional contracts and services. This is the only pool Bridge is paid from.

Revenue

Five streams, none of them a rate on an emergency.

Stream one

Institutional subscription

Organisations pay a recurring fee for the Bridge infrastructure: the assessment, the recovery planning, the participant experience and the reporting.

Stream two

Program administration

Organisations pay for the operational work of running a fund: eligibility, case handling, disbursement coordination, reconciliation and impact reporting.

Stream three

Enterprise programs

Large employers and institutions buy customised programs with their own eligibility rules, branding, integrations and reporting requirements.

Stream four

Financial institution partnerships

Banks and credit unions integrate a Bridge-style resilience program into their own services as a responsible-finance product and engagement channel.

Stream five

Optional service fees

Any consumer-facing fee would have to be transparent, legally compliant and validated through product research before it existed. In the illustrative model on this page it is set at zero.

The constraint

What the model must not do

The business model deliberately avoids depending on maximum interest revenue from financially vulnerable users. If growth ever required more repeat borrowing, the product would be working against its own measured outcomes.

Bridge is a concept designed and submitted for the Graphiques Innovation Challenge. It is a proposal, not a product: it is not a live financial service, it does not lend money, it has no users, partners or revenue, and nothing here is financial advice. The Bridge Resilience Profile is a conceptual product framework for financial resilience planning. It is not a credit score and does not represent a lending decision. Every scenario, dashboard and pilot figure on this site is clearly labelled as illustrative. External statistics carry a named source, a year and a link. Any real implementation would require appropriate financial, legal, regulatory, compliance, consumer protection, privacy and risk review.

30 to 45 seconds · The intervention

Most financial products help people transact. Bridge is designed to help people recover.

Traditional credit asks whether a person can repay. Bridge adds a second question: what would help this person recover? That question changes the product from a loan into a recovery plan with support attached.

Success is not money distributed. It is people who recovered.
