From concept to pilot

What would actually have to be true.

Bridge is a concept. This page is the honest list of everything between the concept and a working pilot, including the parts that are difficult.

Roadmap

Four phases, twelve months to a decision.

Phase one0 to 3 months

Research

  • User research with people who have recently absorbed a shock
  • Financial behaviour research
  • Partner discovery across employers, universities and nonprofits
  • Regulatory analysis by jurisdiction
  • Product design
  • Risk assessment
Phase two3 to 6 months

Pilot

  • One employer, one university, one nonprofit
  • Deliberately small and closely watched
  • Measure adoption and user experience
  • Measure recovery and repayment
  • Measure savings rebuilding
  • Measure the real operational cost per case
Phase three6 to 12 months

Optimisation

  • Refine eligibility against what the pilot showed
  • Adjust the support structure
  • Rework the participant experience
  • Strengthen risk management
  • Simplify partner workflows
  • Harden impact measurement
Phase four12 months and beyond

Expansion

  • More employers
  • More universities
  • Nonprofit networks
  • Financial institutions
  • Community programs
  • Only where the outcome measures held up

Phase two is the phase that decides everything. A pilot that produces good repayment and poor savings rebuilding would mean Bridge had built a small loan product with extra steps, and that would be worth knowing early.

Operational requirements

Six things a real Bridge would need before day one.

A financial partner

Appropriate regulated financial infrastructure. Bridge would not hold or move money on its own account. The partner carries the licences, and the product is designed around what they can actually do.

Compliance review

Lending, payments, consumer protection, privacy, fair lending, disclosure, and both state and federal requirements. Reviewed by jurisdiction, not assumed to transfer.

Security

Protection of financial and personal data, with the controls in place before any participant data exists rather than after.

Fraud prevention

Identity and transaction controls proportionate to the amounts involved, designed so they do not exclude the people the program is for.

Customer support

Human support for complex and vulnerable situations. Some of the cases this product will meet cannot be handled by a screen, and pretending otherwise would be the failure mode.

Impact measurement

A reliable framework in place before the pilot starts, so outcomes are measured rather than reconstructed afterwards.

Risk analysis

Six ways this could go wrong.

Named plainly, with the mitigation next to each one. A risk register that only lists comfortable risks is not a risk register.

Risks and mitigations for a Bridge implementation
RiskWhat could happenMitigation
Over-borrowing People treat Bridge as a recurring source of money rather than as emergency support, and the product becomes the problem it was built to prevent. Affordability limits, emergency-use limits, recovery monitoring, savings pathways, and repeat use that triggers review and human contact instead of a larger limit.
Fraud False emergencies drain program capital that was meant for real ones. Identity verification, transaction controls, and delivery through organisations with an existing relationship to the participant.
Regulatory complexity Small-dollar credit is heavily regulated, and the rules differ by state and by country. Regulated financial partners, jurisdiction-specific legal review, and a deliberately narrow first pilot.
Insufficient funding Demand exceeds the fund and people who qualify are turned away. Institutional funding pools, diversified sponsors, published program limits, transparent eligibility, and recovered capital returning to the pool.
Low adoption The program exists and nobody knows about it. Research on student basic needs found most students were unaware of the supports available to them. Distribution through trusted organisations, surfaced at the moment of need rather than in an onboarding pack.
Inability to repay Some participants still lack the capacity to repay, and a recovery plan becomes another obligation they cannot meet. Affordability assessment before support, conservative support amounts, hardship pathways, and referral rather than lending where the numbers do not work.

The adoption figure is from The Hope Center's 2023 to 2024 Student Basic Needs Survey. Full citation on the research page.

Privacy by design

Five commitments about data.

A product that asks about someone's worst financial month has to be careful with the answer.

  • Collect less. Only what the service actually needs to work.
  • Explain. Say why each piece of information is being asked for, at the point it is asked.
  • Protect. Secure financial information as the default, not as a setting.
  • Control. Give people visibility into what is held about them and what it is used for.
  • Never exploit. Do not sell sensitive financial information. Not to advertisers, not to data brokers, not as a growth strategy.

Accessibility

Built to be usable under stress.

People arrive at this product on a bad day, often on a phone, sometimes on a slow connection. That is the design condition, not the edge case.

  • Full keyboard navigation with visible focus states throughout
  • Screen reader support through real landmarks, headings and labels
  • Text and interface contrast measured against the canvas rather than estimated
  • Reduced-motion preferences honoured, live and in both directions
  • Accessible form labels and thumb-sized controls on touch devices
  • Financial information never depends on colour alone. Every state carries a word as well
  • Responsive layouts with no horizontal scrolling, and the simulator built to work on a phone

The standing disclaimer. Bridge is a concept product. Any real implementation would require appropriate financial, legal, regulatory, compliance, consumer protection, privacy and risk review before a single participant used it.

Bridge is a concept designed and submitted for the Graphiques Innovation Challenge. It is a proposal, not a product: it is not a live financial service, it does not lend money, it has no users, partners or revenue, and nothing here is financial advice. The Bridge Resilience Profile is a conceptual product framework for financial resilience planning. It is not a credit score and does not represent a lending decision. Every scenario, dashboard and pilot figure on this site is clearly labelled as illustrative. External statistics carry a named source, a year and a link. Any real implementation would require appropriate financial, legal, regulatory, compliance, consumer protection, privacy and risk review.

30 to 45 seconds · The intervention

Most financial products help people transact. Bridge is designed to help people recover.

Traditional credit asks whether a person can repay. Bridge adds a second question: what would help this person recover? That question changes the product from a loan into a recovery plan with support attached.

Twelve weeks with a plan, instead of a year without one.

60 to 75 seconds · The business

Organisations fund resilience. Bridge runs it.

Bridge is business to business to consumer. Employers, universities, nonprofits and financial institutions establish a Resilience Fund and pay for the platform that runs it. Revenue comes from institutional contracts, not from interest charged to people in difficulty.

75 to 90 seconds · The impact

Success is not money distributed. It is people who recovered.

Bridge measures emergency resolution, recovery completion, savings rebuilt, repeat dependence, avoided high-cost credit, and change in the Resilience Profile. A pilot with one employer, one university and one nonprofit could test all six inside twelve months.
