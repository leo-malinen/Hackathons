Chapter one

The shock

A small expense rarely stays small.

The original problem is an $850 repair. The larger problem is what a person has to do to cover it, and what that choice costs for the rest of the year.

Bridge is built on one assumption: financial stress is not proof of irresponsible behaviour. Unexpected expenses happen to careful people.

Chapters two and three

The gap, and the bridge

Eight steps from the shock to a stronger position.

Bridge treats a financial emergency as a recovery problem rather than a lending opportunity. That single change reorders the whole product.

03

06

07

Rebuild

Savings restart alongside repayment, not after it.

08

Strengthen

The next shock lands on a buffer instead of on nothing.

Steps five and six describe a regulated activity. Any real Bridge would deliver them through an appropriate financial partner, after legal, compliance and consumer protection review. See Implementation.

The central innovation

One extra question changes the entire product.

“Can this person repay?”

The question traditional credit is built around. It is a good question, and on its own it produces a loan, an interest rate, and a schedule that may or may not fit the person's month.

“What would help this person recover?”

The question Bridge adds. It produces a support amount sized to the gap, a repayment plan sized to real capacity, a savings restart, and a way to tell whether any of it worked.

Most financial products help people transact. Bridge is designed to help people recover.
The goal isn't to keep people borrowing. The goal is to help them stop needing emergency support.

Try it

Press and hold to span the gap.

A gap does not close on its own, and it does not close instantly. Hold the button and watch what a structured recovery actually involves. Let go early and the span eases back down, because a plan that stops halfway leaves the person where they started.

  1. Assess The gap is measured against income, essentials and existing obligations.
  2. Support Support covers the shortfall, sized to the gap rather than to a credit limit.
  3. Recover Repayment runs on a schedule built around what the month can carry.
  4. Rebuild A small savings contribution runs alongside repayment from week four.
  5. Cross The person reaches the far side with a buffer, not just a cleared balance.

Bridge is a concept designed and submitted for the Graphiques Innovation Challenge. It is a proposal, not a product: it is not a live financial service, it does not lend money, it has no users, partners or revenue, and nothing here is financial advice. The Bridge Resilience Profile is a conceptual product framework for financial resilience planning. It is not a credit score and does not represent a lending decision. Every scenario, dashboard and pilot figure on this site is clearly labelled as illustrative. External statistics carry a named source, a year and a link. Any real implementation would require appropriate financial, legal, regulatory, compliance, consumer protection, privacy and risk review.

Organisations fund resilience. Bridge runs it.

Bridge is business to business to consumer. Employers, universities, nonprofits and financial institutions establish a Resilience Fund and pay for the platform that runs it. Revenue comes from institutional contracts, not from interest charged to people in difficulty.
